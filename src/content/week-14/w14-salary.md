## Why this matters for a PM

Getting an offer is the goal, but understanding it is what protects your next few years. Indian tech offers mix fixed pay, variable pay, bonuses, stock and benefits, and the headline “CTC” number can be misleading. Knowing the components, how to compare offers, and how to negotiate politely can change your take-home pay meaningfully, and negotiation itself is a skill PMs use daily.

> [!NOTE]
> Salary ranges change with the market, company and city, and depend on your background. This lesson explains structure and approach, not specific numbers. Use current, reliable sources (recent offers from people you know, company-specific discussions, and salary-data platforms) to calibrate.

## Core concepts

### CTC and its parts

**CTC (cost to company)** is the total annual amount a company spends on you. It typically includes:
1. **Fixed salary (base):** monthly pay before deductions; the most reliable part. Often split into basic, HRA (house rent allowance), special allowance and others for tax purposes.
2. **Variable pay / performance bonus:** paid based on company and individual performance; may be annual or quarterly. Ask what percentage is typically paid out.
3. **Joining bonus (sign-on):** one-time, sometimes with a clawback (you repay it if you leave within a period, often 12 months).
4. **Retention bonus:** paid after staying a certain time.
5. **Equity:**
   - **ESOPs (employee stock options)** at startups: the right to buy shares at a fixed **strike price** after **vesting**. Value depends on the company’s future and on being able to sell (liquidity events like buybacks, acquisitions or IPOs).
   - **RSUs (restricted stock units)** at public companies: actual shares granted over a vesting schedule.
   - **Vesting schedule:** commonly over 4 years, often with a **1-year cliff** (nothing vests before 12 months, then a chunk vests).
6. **Employer PF contribution:** Provident Fund (retirement savings) contribution, part of CTC but not in-hand.
7. **Gratuity:** a statutory benefit paid after (typically) 5 years of service; sometimes included in CTC.
8. **Insurance and benefits:** health insurance (and cover for family), meal cards, learning budgets, wellness, WFH allowances. Sometimes included in CTC.

### In-hand vs. CTC

Your **in-hand** (take-home) monthly pay = fixed salary − employee PF − professional tax − income tax (TDS). It can be much lower than CTC ÷ 12, especially when CTC includes variable pay, equity, PF and gratuity. Always ask for the **CTC breakup** and estimate in-hand.

### Comparing offers

Compare on a like-for-like basis:
- **Guaranteed cash:** fixed pay + any guaranteed bonuses.
- **Expected cash:** + realistic variable payout (ask historical payout rates).
- **Equity:** estimate cautiously; for early-stage ESOPs, consider them a potential upside, not certain money. Ask about the number of options, strike price, latest valuation per share, vesting, cliff, and buyback history.
- **Role quality:** scope, learning, manager, product area, brand, growth path. For a first PM role, **role quality and learning often matter more than a small pay difference**.
- **Stability and risk:** startup runway, funding stage.
- **Benefits:** health insurance coverage, leave, flexibility.

### Negotiation basics

- **Don’t share your current salary early** if you can avoid it; many companies ask. You can say: “I’d prefer to focus on the value I bring to this role. Could you share the budgeted range?” If pressed, be honest; never inflate numbers.
- **Expected salary question:** research the market range for the role and company type, then give a range anchored slightly above your target, or ask for their range first.
- **Negotiate after the offer**, when they’ve decided they want you.
- **Be polite, specific and appreciative:** “I’m excited about this role. Based on my research and another process I’m in, I was hoping for a fixed salary closer to ₹X. Is there flexibility?”
- **Negotiate the whole package:** fixed pay, joining bonus (useful to cover lost bonuses/notice period buyout), equity, start date, learning budget, title.
- **Use real leverage only:** competing offers can help; never bluff.
- **Get everything in writing** (offer letter with breakup).
- **Know your walk-away point** before negotiating.

### Notice periods

In India, notice periods are commonly 30–90 days. Companies may ask you to join sooner; some offer a **notice period buyout** (they pay your former employer to release you early). Clarify your notice period and any buyout policy with your current employer.

### Career-switcher considerations

- Your first PM role may pay similar to (or occasionally less than) your current technical role; think long term: PM career growth can outpace the difference.
- **Title and scope** (APM vs. PM) affect future offers; ask about promotion criteria and timelines.
- **Don’t accept on the spot**; ask for 2–3 days to review.

## Worked example

**Comparing two offers (illustrative structures, rupees in lakh per annum, LPA):**

| Component | Offer A: growth-stage fintech APM | Offer B: early-stage AI startup PM |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed | 20 | 18 |
| Variable (target) | 2 (paid ~80% historically) | 0 |
| Joining bonus | 1.5 (12-month clawback) | 0 |
| ESOPs | 1,000 options, strike ₹100, latest value ₹600/share, 4-yr vesting, 1-yr cliff | 0.5% of company, early stage, 4-yr vesting, 1-yr cliff |
| Employer PF + gratuity (in CTC) | ~1.5 | ~1.2 |
| Headline CTC | 26+ | 19.2 + ESOPs |

**Analysis:**
- **Guaranteed cash (year 1):** A: 20 + 1.5 bonus = 21.5; B: 18.
- **Expected cash:** A adds ~1.6 variable → ~23.
- **Equity:** A’s options have a paper gain of (600 − 100) × 1,000 = ₹5 lakh total over 4 years (~₹1.25 lakh/year), if liquidity exists. B’s 0.5% could be worth a lot or nothing; very uncertain.
- **Role:** B offers broader AI product scope (strong for AI PM growth); A offers structured mentorship and a strong brand.
- **Decision approach:** If learning and AI focus are top priorities and finances allow, B may be better long term; if stability and mentorship matter more, A. Negotiate: ask A for fixed 21 and B for a joining bonus or more ESOPs.

## Step by step

When you receive an offer:

1. **Thank them and ask for the written offer with full CTC breakup.**
2. **Clarify:** variable payout history, bonus clawbacks, equity details (number, strike, valuation, vesting, cliff, buyback history), benefits, notice-period expectations.
3. **Estimate in-hand monthly pay.**
4. **Compare offers** on guaranteed cash, expected cash, equity (cautiously) and role quality.
5. **Decide your priorities and walk-away point.**
6. **Negotiate once, politely**, with specific asks and real reasons.
7. **Get final terms in writing** before resigning.
8. **Plan your notice period** and resignation professionally.

## Common mistakes

1. **Comparing headline CTCs** without breakup.
2. **Treating ESOPs as cash.**
3. **Accepting on the spot** without reviewing.
4. **Inflating current salary** (dishonest; can be verified and cost you the offer).
5. **Aggressive or repeated negotiation** that sours relationships.
6. **Ignoring role quality** for a small pay difference in a first PM role.

## How it shows up in interviews

> [!INTERVIEW]
> **“What are your salary expectations?”**
> Outline: express enthusiasm → ask for their range if possible → otherwise give a researched range for the role and city → note you’re flexible depending on the full package and scope.

> [!INTERVIEW]
> **“What’s your notice period?”**
> Outline: state it honestly; mention any flexibility (e.g., possible early release or buyout) only if real.

## Practice exercise

**Task (25 minutes):** Using this illustrative offer, calculate guaranteed year-1 cash and write a polite negotiation message: fixed ₹18 LPA, variable ₹2 LPA (historically 70% paid), joining bonus ₹1 L (12-month clawback), 800 ESOPs at strike ₹50, latest valuation ₹300/share, 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff. You have another process at final round.

```answer Reveal a model answer
**Guaranteed year-1 cash:** fixed 18 + joining bonus 1 = **₹19 L** (bonus repayable if you leave within 12 months).
**Expected year-1 cash:** + variable 2 × 70% = 1.4 → **≈ ₹20.4 L**.
**ESOPs (paper value):** (300 − 50) × 800 = ₹2 L total over 4 years, nothing before the 1-year cliff; liquidity uncertain.

**Negotiation message:**
“Thank you for the offer. I’m genuinely excited about the role and the team’s work on merchant growth. I’m in the final round with another company, and based on my research for APM roles in Bengaluru, I was hoping for a fixed salary closer to ₹20 LPA. If fixed pay is constrained, a higher joining bonus to cover the bonus I’d forgo by leaving now would also help. Is there any flexibility? I’d love to make this work.”
```

## Key takeaways

- **CTC** bundles fixed pay, variable, bonuses, equity, PF, gratuity and benefits; **in-hand** is much lower than CTC ÷ 12.
- Always get the **written breakup**; ask about **variable payout history, clawbacks, vesting and cliffs**.
- Compare on **guaranteed cash, expected cash, cautious equity value and role quality**.
- Negotiate **after the offer**, **politely and honestly**, across the **whole package**.
- For a first PM role, **learning and scope** often matter more than a small pay gap.
