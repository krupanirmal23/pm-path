## Why this matters for a PM

Two candidates do the same work. One writes “Worked on onboarding improvements.” The other writes “Increased onboarding completion from 41% to 58% by redesigning KYC upload, tested with 12 users.” Only one gets the interview. Impact bullets are where your resume wins or loses, and the same skill shapes your LinkedIn, cover notes and interview answers.

## Core concepts

### The XYZ formula

A widely used structure (popularised by Google recruiters):

> **Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z].**

- **X:** the outcome or accomplishment.
- **Y:** the measurement (number, percentage, time, money, scale).
- **Z:** what *you* did (your actions, methods, decisions).

Example: “Reduced checkout drop-off by 18% (X, Y) by removing a redundant address step and testing the new flow with 8 users (Z).”

Variants are fine as long as the parts are there. Often you’ll lead with a verb:
> “Cut support tickets about refunds by 30% by adding a refund tracker with clear timelines.”

### Verbs that signal ownership

Prefer specific, active verbs:
- **Built / Shipped / Launched** (delivery)
- **Increased / Reduced / Grew / Cut** (outcomes)
- **Designed / Defined / Specified** (product definition)
- **Analysed / Diagnosed / Identified** (data and discovery)
- **Led / Aligned / Drove** (influence) — use only if true; be specific about how.
- **Interviewed / Tested / Validated** (research)

Avoid weak openers: “Responsible for”, “Worked on”, “Helped with”, “Involved in”, “Assisted”.

### Finding numbers when you think you have none

Numbers come in many forms:
- **Scale:** users, customers, requests, documents, teams, markets (“for 200+ sales engineers”).
- **Change:** before/after percentages or absolutes (“from 66% to 90%”).
- **Time:** saved, reduced, delivered (“in 3 weeks”, “cut from 2 days to 4 hours”).
- **Money:** saved, earned, cost per user (“₹1.8 per user per month”).
- **Quality:** accuracy, error rates, satisfaction (“91% agreement with human labels”).
- **Effort/rigour:** interviews run, tests conducted (“12 user interviews”, “5 usability tests”).

If you can’t measure the outcome, show **scale and rigour** honestly. Never invent. If a number is an estimate, be ready to explain how you estimated it.

### Show product thinking in bullets

PM bullets are strongest when they reveal **judgement**, not just execution:
- The **problem** you found (“found 60% of drop-off happened at KYC upload on low-end phones”).
- The **decision** you made and why (“prioritised UPI auto-fetch over a redesign based on RICE”).
- The **trade-off** (“shipped behind a flag to 10% first”).

### Tailoring bullets

Reorder and tweak bullets to match each role:
- For a **growth PM** role: emphasise activation, funnels, experiments.
- For an **AI PM** role: emphasise evals, prompts, cost, guardrails, AI UX.
- For a **B2B/platform** role: emphasise technical depth, APIs, customer insight.

### Bullets from non-PM experience

Translate your Technical Marketing Engineer work into product-relevant outcomes:
- Customer insight → “Synthesised feedback from 30+ enterprise calls into prioritised requests…”
- Technical depth → “Built demo environments for X features, used by Y engineers…”
- Cross-functional → “Partnered with product and engineering on 3 launches…”
- Communication → “Wrote launch content read by…; reduced escalations by…”

## Worked example

**Turning weak bullets into XYZ bullets (P-projects and TME role):**

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Did user research for an expense app. | Ran 6 discovery interviews and a 23-response screener to find that flatmates avoid asking for money; reframed the product from “splitting” to “settling without asking”. |
| Made a dashboard in Mixpanel. | Built a 9-chart activation dashboard on demo data and diagnosed a simulated 15% drop to a sign-up method change, with 4 ranked hypotheses and SQL checks. |
| Built an AI app. | Shipped an LLM expense assistant used by 40+ users in 3 weeks; 68% of AI drafts accepted without edits. |
| Improved the AI. | Raised eval pass rate from 66% to 90% on a 50-example golden set by error-analysing 17 failures and fixing the top two categories. |
| Worked with sales on launches. | Created launch demos and battlecards for 3 security features, adopted by 200+ sales engineers across APAC. |
| Did a teardown of UPI. | Mapped the UPI payment flow across 6 systems, documented 5 failure points and 3 design trade-offs; published with a Postman collection on GitHub. |

## Step by step

Rewriting your bullets:

1. **List every accomplishment** (projects and TME role), one per line, in plain words.
2. **For each, find the number**: scale, change, time, money, quality or rigour.
3. **Write the XYZ version.**
4. **Check the verb** (strong, specific, true).
5. **Cut filler** (“successfully”, “various”, “in order to”).
6. **Keep to 1–2 lines.**
7. **Rank bullets** by relevance to the target role; keep the top 2–3 per entry.
8. **Read each aloud and ask “so what?”**. If there’s no clear answer, strengthen or cut.

## Common mistakes

1. **Activities without outcomes** (“Conducted interviews”).
2. **Invented or inflated numbers.**
3. **Vague verbs** (“helped”, “worked on”).
4. **Team results claimed as personal** without clarifying your part.
5. **Jargon** recruiters won’t understand (internal project names, acronyms).
6. **Too many bullets**: 6 weak bullets hide 2 great ones.

## How it shows up in interviews

> [!INTERVIEW]
> **“How did you measure that?”** (about any bullet)
> Outline: definition of the metric → baseline and after → time period → data source → caveats (small sample, estimate) → what you learned. Being honest about limits builds credibility.

> [!INTERVIEW]
> **“What was your specific contribution?”**
> Outline: separate team outcome from your actions → name decisions you made → evidence (docs, analysis) → collaborators’ roles.

2. Responsible for customer feedback.
3. Worked on a chatbot project.
4. Created reports for management.
5. Participated in product launches.
6. Did A/B testing.

```answer Reveal model answers
2. Turned 400+ app reviews and 25 support tickets into 5 ranked problem themes, which set the next quarter’s top two roadmap items.
3. Launched a support chatbot answering order-status questions from live order data; resolved 46% of queries without an agent in the first month.
4. Built a weekly self-serve dashboard of 8 metrics that replaced manual reports, saving the team ~4 hours a week.
5. Planned and ran a staged rollout (5% → 100%) for a scheduled-delivery feature, with guardrails that caught a payment bug at 5% before wider release.
6. Designed and analysed a 2-week A/B test of reorder prompts (n = 28,000); lifted 7-day repeat orders by 2.1 percentage points with no drop in order value.
```

```quiz
- [x] Lead with evals, prompt and model decisions, cost per user and guardrails :: Correct. Match the role’s priorities.
```

## Key takeaways

- Use **XYZ**: outcome (X), measurement (Y), your actions (Z).
- Start with **strong, specific verbs**; drop “responsible for” and “worked on”.
- Numbers can be **scale, change, time, money, quality or rigour**; **never invent** them.
- Show **judgement**: the problem, the decision, the trade-off.
- **Tailor** bullet order and emphasis to each role; be ready to **explain every number**.
