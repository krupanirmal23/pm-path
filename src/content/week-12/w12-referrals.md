## Why this matters for a PM

In Bengaluru’s competitive PM market, a referral can be the difference between your resume being read and being lost among hundreds of applications. Referred candidates are often prioritised by recruiters, and many companies reward employees for successful referrals. For career switchers, referrals also add trust that a resume alone can’t. Your deliverable: 25 referral messages this week, and 10 a week after that.

## Core concepts

### What a referral is (and isn’t)

A **referral** is when an employee submits or recommends you for a role through the company’s internal system or directly to a recruiter. It usually means:
- Your application gets seen (often faster).
- The recruiter knows someone internal vouched for, or at least forwarded, you.

It doesn’t guarantee an interview, and referrers have different levels of conviction: a strong referral (“I worked with her”) is different from “a stranger asked me”. Both help.

### Who to ask

In order of strength:
1. **People who know your work:** ex-colleagues, managers, classmates, people from Palo Alto Networks who moved to product companies.
2. **Alumni** from your college at target companies.
3. **People in your extended network:** friends of friends, community members.
4. **Thoughtful cold outreach:** PMs at the company whose posts you’ve engaged with, or people in similar backgrounds (ex-engineers or TMEs who became PMs).

Target **PMs, APMs and people in product-adjacent roles** at the company; engineers and others can refer too. Recruiters can be contacted directly as well.

### The ask: make it easy

A referrer is doing you a favour. Make it effortless:
- **Be specific:** the exact role, job ID or link.
- **Be brief:** they’re busy; under 120 words for a first message.
- **Show fit:** 1–2 lines of proof relevant to the role.
- **Give them what they need:** resume link, a 2-line summary they can paste into the referral form.
- **Make saying no easy:** “Totally fine if not.”

### Message structure

1. **Personal opener** (how you know them, or a genuine specific reference: their post, shared college, similar background).
2. **The ask** (the specific role with link).
3. **Why you fit** (one line of strongest proof).
4. **Make it easy** (attachments/links; offer a 2-line blurb).
5. **Graceful close.**

### Cold vs. warm messages

- **Warm** (they know you): you can ask directly.
- **Cold** (they don’t): consider a two-step approach: first a short message asking for 10 minutes of advice or a specific question about the role/team; after a useful exchange, ask if they’d be comfortable referring you. Some people are happy to refer directly; read the situation.

### Follow-ups

- If no reply in **5 working days**, send one polite follow-up.
- If still nothing, move on (no third nudge), and stay engaged with their content.
- When someone refers you, **thank them** and **update them** on the outcome later. It builds relationships for the future.

### Tracking

Log every outreach in PM Path’s **Job Search** tracker (referral contact field) and the **outreach counter** (target: 5 a day). Track response rate: if it’s very low (under ~10%), improve personalisation or targeting.

### Etiquette and ethics

- Don’t spam dozens of people at the same company the same day.
- Don’t exaggerate how well you know someone.
- Don’t send resume attachments to strangers without context.
- Respect “no” and silence.

## Worked example

**Warm (ex-colleague now at a fintech):**
> Hi Arjun, hope the new role at Razorpay is going well! I saw an Associate PM opening on the Payments team (link). I’ve been preparing for product for a while; recently I shipped an AI expense app to 40+ users and did a technical teardown of UPI payments, which feels very relevant. Would you be open to referring me? I’ve attached my resume and a 2-line summary you can paste. Completely fine if it’s not a good fit. Thanks either way!

**Cold (alumnus, two-step):**
> Hi Sneha, I’m a fellow [college] alum (2022) and a Technical Marketing Engineer moving into product. I enjoyed your post on how your team runs pricing experiments. I’m applying for the APM role at Groww and had one question: does the team look for analytics depth, or more product sense, from APMs? Any pointer would really help.
> *(After a reply:)* Thanks, that’s really helpful. I’ve been doing exactly that kind of work (an analytics case with SQL and an experiment plan). Would you be comfortable referring me for the role (link)? Resume and a short blurb attached; no worries at all if not.

**2-line blurb for the referrer:**
> “Krupa is a Technical Marketing Engineer at Palo Alto Networks moving into product. She has shipped an AI product to 40+ users and completed product case studies in discovery, analytics and payments architecture.”

## Step by step

Running this week’s 25 referral messages:

1. **Pick 25 target companies** (APM role finding lesson).
2. **For each, find 2 people** (LinkedIn search: company + “product manager” + your college or ex-company).
3. **Prioritise warm connections.**
4. **Find a genuine personal hook** for each (post, shared background, shared college).
5. **Write messages** with the 5-part structure (template in PM Path Templates: “Referral message”).
6. **Attach or link** your resume and portfolio; include the 2-line blurb.
7. **Send 5 per day**; log each in the Job Tracker and outreach counter.
8. **Follow up once** after 5 working days.
9. **Thank and update** everyone who helps.
10. **Review weekly**: reply rate, referral rate; improve messages that don’t work.

## Common mistakes

1. **Generic copy-paste messages** (“Hi, please refer me”).
2. **No specific role or link.**
3. **Long life stories** in the first message.
4. **Asking strangers for a referral before any context.**
5. **Multiple follow-ups** that feel like pressure.
6. **Not tracking**, so you lose count and double-message people.

## How it shows up in interviews

> [!INTERVIEW]
> Referrers sometimes give you useful context (“the team cares a lot about experimentation”). Use it to prepare, and mention your connection appropriately if asked how you heard about the role. Never misrepresent how well you know them.

> [!INTERVIEW]
> **Behavioural angle:** “Tell me about a time you had to influence someone without authority.” Your outreach experience (getting busy strangers to help) can be a light, honest example of persuasion and persistence, though product examples are stronger.

## Key takeaways

- Referrals get your application **seen**; warm referrers are strongest.
- Message structure: **personal hook → specific ask → one line of fit → make it easy → graceful close**.
- For cold contacts, consider a **two-step** approach.
- **One follow-up after 5 working days**; always **thank and update** helpers.
- **Track** outreach (5 a day) and **improve** messages if reply rates are low.
