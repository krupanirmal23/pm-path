## Why this matters for a PM

In India’s tech job market, LinkedIn is where recruiters search, referrers check you out before forwarding your resume, and hiring managers look you up before interviews. A clear profile turns cold outreach into warm conversations, and posting about your product work builds credibility faster than any certificate. Your Week 12 deliverable includes a rewritten headline and About section.

## Core concepts

### How your profile gets found and judged

- **Recruiters search** by keywords (titles, skills, location): “Associate Product Manager Bengaluru”, “AI product”, “SQL”, “A/B testing”. Your **headline**, **About**, **experience** and **skills** fields feed this search.
- **People judge in seconds**: photo, headline, the first lines of About, and your featured items.
- **Activity signals:** recent posts and comments show you’re engaged and thoughtful.

### Profile sections that matter most

1. **Photo:** clear, friendly, professional (good light, plain background, face visible). No need for a formal suit.
2. **Banner:** optional; a simple image with your focus (“Product · AI · Fintech”) works.
3. **Headline (220 characters max):** the most important line. Shows in search results and next to every comment you make.
4. **About:** your story in 3–5 short paragraphs (the written version of your career story).
5. **Featured:** pin your best work: portfolio, P4 live app, a case study, a strong post.
6. **Experience:** your TME role with product-lens bullets; add a **Projects** entry or a “Product projects” experience entry for P1–P4 (be honest: “Independent product projects”, not a fake company).
7. **Skills:** add relevant skills (Product Management, Product Discovery, SQL, A/B Testing, Product Analytics, Generative AI, Prompt Engineering, LLM Evaluation) and pin the top three.
8. **Open to work:** set job preferences (titles, Bengaluru, remote/hybrid). You can make it visible to recruiters only.
9. **Custom URL:** linkedin.com/in/yourname-pm style.

### Writing a strong headline

Formula: **Target role/identity | Proof | Domain/strength**

Examples:
- “Aspiring APM | Shipped an AI expense app to 40+ users | Ex-Technical Marketing Engineer, Palo Alto Networks”
- “Product builder → APM/AI PM | Discovery, SQL, evals | 4 product case studies | Bengaluru”

Avoid: “Seeking opportunities”, a list of buzzwords, or only your current title (“Technical Marketing Engineer at Palo Alto Networks”) if you’re repositioning.

**Honesty rule:** don’t call yourself “Product Manager” if you haven’t held the role. “Aspiring APM”, “Product builder” or “Transitioning to product” are honest and common.

### Writing the About section

Structure:
1. **Hook (first 2 lines, visible before “see more”):** who you are and your strongest proof.
2. **Story:** pivot from TME to product (short).
3. **Proof:** 3 bullets with projects and numbers.
4. **What you’re looking for:** roles, domains, location.
5. **Call to action:** “Happy to chat about product, AI evals or fintech. Message me.”

Keep it scannable: short paragraphs, a few bullets, no walls of text.

### Posting: building in public

Posting about your learning and projects is one of the most effective ways for career switchers to get noticed:
- **What to post:** teardown insights, a chart from your SQL analysis, lessons from 6 user interviews, your eval “66% → 90%” story, a P4 launch post asking for users, a failure and what you learned.
- **Format:** a strong first line, 5–10 short lines, one image (diagram, screenshot), a question at the end. Plain language beats jargon.
- **Frequency:** 1–2 posts a week during Weeks 12–15+. Consistency matters more than virality.
- **Comment thoughtfully** on posts by PMs at target companies: add a perspective, not “Great post!”. This gets your name seen.
- **Respect privacy:** no confidential employer information; anonymise interview participants.

### Connections and networking

- Connect with PMs, APMs, recruiters and alumni at target companies, with a short personalised note (Referral outreach lesson).
- Engage before asking: comment on their posts, mention something specific.
- Join relevant groups and communities (product communities in Bengaluru, AI product groups).

## Step by step

Rewriting your LinkedIn this week:

1. **Photo and custom URL.**
2. **Headline** with role/identity, proof and strengths (honest titles).
3. **About** using the 5-part structure; check the first 2 lines.
4. **Featured**: portfolio, P4 app, a case study, a post.
5. **Experience**: product-lens bullets for your TME role; an honest projects entry for P1–P4.
6. **Skills**: add and pin the top 3 relevant skills.
7. **Open to work** preferences (recruiters only if you prefer).
8. **Write your first post** (e.g., your P4 launch asking for users, or an eval lesson).
9. **Connect with 25 people** at target companies with personalised notes.
10. **Ask 2 PMs to review** your profile alongside your resume (checkpoint).

## Common mistakes

1. **Claiming a PM title** you didn’t hold.
2. **Empty About section** or a wall of text.
3. **Headline of buzzwords** (“Passionate | Innovative | Visionary”).
4. **Never posting**, so you’re invisible.
5. **Generic connection requests** with no note.
6. **Profile and resume telling different stories.**

## How it shows up in interviews

> [!INTERVIEW]
> Interviewers often read your LinkedIn and posts before calling. A post about your eval process or a teardown gives them easy, positive conversation starters (“I saw your post about UPI failure points; tell me more”). Make sure everything posted is something you can discuss in depth.

> [!INTERVIEW]
> **“I saw you’re transitioning into product. How have you prepared?”**
> Outline: point to concrete projects and outcomes (also visible in Featured), the skills you built, and what you learned from real users.

## Practice exercise

**Task (40 minutes):** Write your new headline (3 options), About section (≤ 250 words), and your first post (≤ 150 words) about one P-project insight. Ask a friend which headline they’d click.

```answer Reveal a model answer (post)
“I tested my AI expense assistant on 50 real messages. It passed 66%.

Most failures weren’t ‘AI being dumb’. They fell into two buckets:
• Hinglish amounts like ‘dedh hazaar’ read as 1,000 (should be 1,500)
• Unknown names silently matched to the wrong flatmate

Two small changes fixed most of it:
1. A few Hinglish examples in the prompt
2. A rule: if a name isn’t in the group, ask instead of guessing

Same 50 messages, version 2: 90%.

Lesson for anyone building with LLMs: label your outputs, group the failures, fix the biggest group first. Prompt tweaking without error analysis is guessing.

If you share flat expenses in Bengaluru, I’d love you to try it (link in comments). What’s the weirdest way you’ve split a bill?”
```

## Key takeaways

- Recruiters **search by keywords** and judge **headline, photo, About hook and Featured** in seconds.
- Headline formula: **target role | proof | strengths**, always **honest**.
- About: **hook → story → proof bullets → what you want → call to action**.
- **Post 1–2 times a week** about real work; **comment thoughtfully** on target PMs’ posts.
- Keep LinkedIn and resume **consistent**, and be ready to discuss anything you post.
