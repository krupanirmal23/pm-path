## Why this matters for a PM

Recruiters often spend well under a minute on a first resume scan, sometimes after an applicant tracking system has filtered it. Your resume’s only job is to earn a conversation. For a career switcher into product, structure matters even more: it must make your **product evidence** (P1–P4) and **transferable experience** obvious at a glance. You’ll build PM and AI PM versions this week.

## Core concepts

### What recruiters and hiring managers look for

In a first scan, they ask:
1. **Can this person do product work?** Evidence of discovery, prioritisation, shipping, metrics.
2. **Impact, not activity:** numbers and outcomes.
3. **Relevant signals:** domain (fintech, consumer, B2B), technical fluency, AI experience for AI PM roles.
4. **Clarity:** can they communicate crisply? (The resume is your first writing sample.)
5. **Fit for level:** APM/entry PM roles expect potential, learning speed and evidence of ownership, not years of PM titles.

### Applicant tracking systems (ATS)

Many companies use an **ATS** (software that stores and filters applications). Some search resumes for keywords. Practical implications:
- Use a **simple, single-column layout** with standard headings (Experience, Projects, Education, Skills).
- Avoid text inside images, complex tables, multi-column templates, headers/footers holding key information, and unusual fonts.
- Submit as **PDF** unless asked otherwise (and check it’s text-selectable).
- Mirror important **keywords** from the job description naturally (e.g., “A/B testing”, “SQL”, “PRD”, “LLM evaluation”).

### The structure (one page)

For someone with under ~5 years of experience, **one page** is the norm.

1. **Header:** name, city (Bengaluru), phone, email, LinkedIn URL, portfolio URL. No photo, date of birth, marital status or full address (not needed; reduces bias).
2. **Summary (optional, 2 lines):** who you are and what you’re aiming for, with your strongest proof. Example: “Technical Marketing Engineer turned product builder. Shipped an AI expense assistant used by 40+ flatmates; 4 product case studies in discovery, analytics, technical design and AI evals.”
3. **Product experience / Projects:** for a career switcher, put your **portfolio projects** (P1–P4) near the top, written like jobs: name, one-line context, 2–3 impact bullets each.
4. **Professional experience:** your Technical Marketing Engineer role at Palo Alto Networks, with bullets emphasising product-relevant work: customer insight, technical depth, cross-functional work, launches, measurable outcomes.
5. **Education:** degree, institution, year; relevant coursework only if strong.
6. **Skills:** grouped, not a long list: *Product:* discovery, PRDs, prioritisation, A/B testing. *Data:* SQL, Mixpanel/PostHog, Google Sheets. *Technical:* APIs, Postman, system design basics. *AI:* prompting, RAG, evals, LLM-as-judge. *Tools:* Jira, Figma, Notion.
7. **Optional:** awards, publications, notable side projects, languages spoken (useful for Indian multilingual products).

### Bullet-writing basics (next lesson goes deep)

- Start with a strong **verb** (Led, Built, Shipped, Reduced, Grew, Designed, Analysed).
- Show **impact with numbers** (users, %, ₹, time saved).
- Be **specific** about what *you* did.
- Keep bullets to 1–2 lines.

### PM vs. AI PM versions

Keep one base resume, then tailor:
- **PM version:** lead with P1 (end-to-end product case) and P2 (analytics/experimentation); emphasise discovery, prioritisation, metrics, stakeholder work.
- **AI PM version:** lead with P4 (shipped AI product) and evals; add AI-specific skills (prompting, RAG, evals, guardrails, cost per user); summary mentions AI explicitly.

### Common Indian-market specifics

- **Notice period** isn’t usually on the resume; it comes up in screening calls.
- **CTC (cost to company)** shouldn’t be on the resume.
- **CGPA/percentage:** include if strong or if the company asks (common for APM programs and recent graduates).
- **References:** “available on request” isn’t needed.

## Worked example

**Before (weak):**
> **Technical Marketing Engineer, Palo Alto Networks** (2023–2024)
> - Responsible for creating demos and documentation.
> - Worked with product managers and sales teams.
> - Attended customer calls.

**After (strong):**
> **Technical Marketing Engineer, Palo Alto Networks** · Bengaluru · Jan 2023 – Apr 2024
> - Turned feedback from 30+ enterprise customer calls into 12 prioritised feature requests for the firewall product team; 4 shipped in the next two releases.
> - Built technical demos and launch content for 3 security feature launches, used by 200+ sales engineers globally.
> - Created a troubleshooting guide for a common configuration issue that reduced related escalations from the field (reported by support leads).

**Projects section (top of resume):**
> **FlatSplit AI – AI expense assistant (P4)** · Live with 40+ users
> - Built and launched an LLM-powered app that turns plain-language messages into shared expenses; 68% of AI drafts accepted without edits.
> - Designed a 60-example eval set and validated an LLM judge (91% agreement with human labels); improved pass rate from 66% to 90% through error analysis.
> - Cut cost per user to ₹1.8/month via prompt trimming and model routing; wrote a responsible-AI note covering DPDP-aligned data handling.

(Numbers must be **your real numbers**; never invent them.)

## Step by step

Building your PM resume:

1. **Collect raw material**: every project, number, result and piece of feedback from P1–P4 and your TME role.
2. **Choose a simple template** (Google Docs or Canva single-column; see the tool guide).
3. **Write the header** with portfolio and LinkedIn links (custom LinkedIn URL).
4. **Write project entries** for P1–P4: context line + 2–3 impact bullets each (next lesson for bullets).
5. **Rewrite TME bullets** with a product lens: customer insight, technical depth, cross-functional work, outcomes.
6. **Add education and grouped skills.**
7. **Cut to one page**: remove weak bullets, merge duplicates.
8. **Create the AI PM variant**: reorder projects, adjust summary and skills.
9. **ATS check**: copy-paste the PDF text into a plain text editor; is everything readable and in order?
10. **Get reviews** from 2 working PMs (Week 12 checkpoint) and fix what they flag.

## Common mistakes

1. **Responsibilities instead of results** (“Responsible for…”).
2. **Fancy multi-column templates** that confuse ATS systems.
3. **Two pages** for an early-career PM resume.
4. **Hiding projects at the bottom** when they’re your strongest evidence.
5. **Invented or vague numbers.** Interviewers will ask about every number.
6. **One resume for every job**, ignoring keywords in the posting.

## How it shows up in interviews

> [!INTERVIEW]
> **“Walk me through your resume.”**
> Outline: 90 seconds, chronological story with a thread: technical foundation → customer-facing work sparked interest in *why* things are built → deliberate transition (projects with results) → why this role now. Mention 1–2 numbers. (Week 12 career story lesson.)

> [!INTERVIEW]
> **“Tell me more about this bullet.”**
> Outline: be ready to explain the context, your specific actions, how you measured the result, and what you’d do differently, for every line on your resume.

## Key takeaways

- The resume’s job is to **earn a conversation**; make **product proof** visible in seconds.
- **One page, single column, standard headings, text-based PDF** for ATS.
- Put **P1–P4 projects near the top**; rewrite past roles with a **product lens**.
- Every bullet: **verb + specific action + real, measured result**.
- Keep a base resume with **PM and AI PM variants**, and get **PM reviewers** to critique it.
