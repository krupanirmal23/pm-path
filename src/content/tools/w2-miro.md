## What it is

**Miro** is the online whiteboard you set up in Week 1. This week you’ll use it for two research artefacts: an **affinity map** (to find themes in your interview notes) and an **opportunity solution tree**.

## Why PMs use it

Research synthesis is visual work. Moving sticky notes around helps you see patterns that a list hides. A clean affinity map and OST are also great screenshots for your discovery report and portfolio.

## Account setup (free plan)

You already have an account from Week 1. The free plan allows 3 editable boards. Create a new board for Week 2, or export your Week 1 board as an image and reuse it.

## Walkthrough part 1: affinity map

### 1. Get your notes onto the board
1. Open your notes doc where each line is one observation with a participant code (`P2: "Splitwise lasted one week."`).
2. Copy 30–60 lines (one interview at a time is fine).
3. In Miro, click on the canvas and **paste** (Ctrl/Cmd + V). Miro offers to turn multi-line text into **separate sticky notes**. Choose that option. If your version pastes one big note, use the **sticky note** tool (N) and paste lines into notes in batches.
4. Colour-code by participant or by type. Select notes → choose a colour in the toolbar. Using one colour per participant makes counting people easy.

### 2. Cluster silently
1. Zoom out (Shift + 1 to fit). Drag notes that feel related close together.
2. Don’t create headings yet. Put unclear notes in a “parking lot” area to the side.
3. If two notes fit two clusters, duplicate one (Ctrl/Cmd + D).

### 3. Name the clusters
1. Once a cluster has 3+ notes from 2+ participants, add a large **dark-coloured** sticky above it.
2. Write a user-voice headline: “I’d rather lose money than argue with friends.”
3. Draw a **frame** (F) around each cluster so you can move it as one unit.

### 4. Count and rank
1. On each headline sticky, add the count: “5 of 6 participants”.
2. Arrange frames from strongest to weakest.
3. Add a small “Contradictions” frame for notes that challenge your assumptions.

### 5. Export
1. Select a frame → **⋯** → **Export** → Image. Use these images in your discovery report next to the quotes.

## Walkthrough part 2: opportunity solution tree

### 1. Draw the outcome
1. Create a new frame titled “P1 Opportunity solution tree”.
2. Use the **shape** tool (S) to draw a green rectangle at the top. Type your outcome with baseline and target.

### 2. Add opportunities
1. Below it, add a rectangle for each top theme, reworded as an opportunity (“I don’t want to be the one who asks”).
2. Connect them to the outcome: hover over a shape until a small dot appears on its edge, then drag to the other shape to create a **connector line**.
3. Add sub-opportunities beneath big ones.
4. Highlight your **target opportunity** with a thicker border (select → border width) or a yellow fill.

### 3. Add solutions and experiments
1. Under the target opportunity, add 3+ blue rectangles for solutions.
2. Under each solution, add a purple rectangle with the experiment and its success threshold: “Fake door to 50 users; go if ≥10% tap.”
3. Connect everything with lines.

### 4. Tidy and export
1. Select all → right-click → **Align** and **Distribute** to make it neat.
2. Lock the frame when done (right-click → Lock).
3. Export the frame as an image for your report.

## Tips

- **One participant, one colour** makes “at least 2 quotes from different people” easy to check.
- **Keep sticky text short.** If a note needs a paragraph, it’s probably two observations.
- **Use templates sparingly.** Miro has affinity and OST templates, but building it yourself once teaches you the structure.
- **Name frames clearly** (“Affinity – P1 – v2”) so you can find them later.
- **Screenshot progress** at each stage; a before/after image of clustering makes a nice portfolio visual.
