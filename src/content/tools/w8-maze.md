## What it is

- **Maze** is a remote user testing platform. You import a Figma prototype, write tasks, and share a link; participants complete tasks on their own while Maze records clicks, paths, success rates and time. It has a free plan with limits.
- **Google Meet** (or any video call) lets you run **moderated** tests: the participant shares their screen while using your Figma prototype and thinks aloud.

## Why PMs use it

Usability testing is how you prove (or disprove) that your design works. Moderated tests on Meet give rich “why” insights; Maze gives quick quantitative signals across more people. Your checkpoint (4 of 5 succeed on v2) needs real tests.

## Account setup (free plan)

**Google Meet:** use your Google account. Free calls with up to 100 participants; group calls have a time limit on the free plan, but one-to-one calls are typically longer. Check current limits.

**Maze:**
1. Go to maze.co → **Get started for free**.
2. Sign up with Google. The free plan allows a limited number of active studies and responses per month; check the pricing page for current limits.
3. Connect Figma when prompted (or paste your prototype link later).

## Walkthrough A: moderated tests on Google Meet (recommended for P1)

### 1. Prepare
1. Finalise your test plan: 3–4 scenario tasks with success criteria (from the lesson).
2. Make your Figma prototype link viewable by anyone with the link.
3. Create a notes sheet: rows = participants, columns = tasks (success: ✓ / help / ✗), time, ease (1–5), issues, quotes.
4. Book five 45-minute slots; send calendar invites with the Meet link.

### 2. Run each session
1. Start recording only after consent (Meet recording may require a paid Workspace plan; if unavailable, take notes and use captions or a phone voice recorder with permission).
2. Intro: “We’re testing the design, not you. Please think aloud.”
3. Send the prototype link in chat. Ask them to open it on their **phone** if possible (that’s how real users will use it), and share their screen, or use the laptop if phone sharing is hard.
4. Read one task at a time (also paste it in chat). Observe silently. Note hesitations and quotes.
5. After each task: “On a scale of 1 to 5, how easy was that?”
6. Debrief: hardest part, anything confusing, overall impressions.

### 3. Analyse
1. Fill the success grid; count unaided successes per task.
2. List issues with frequency (x/5) and severity (0–4).
3. Pick the top 3–5 to fix in v2.

## Walkthrough B: unmoderated test in Maze (optional)

### 1. Create a maze
1. In Maze, click **Create new maze** → choose a blank maze or a usability testing template.
2. Add a **Prototype test** block → paste your Figma prototype link → Maze imports the screens.
3. Write a **mission** (task): “Your flatmate says you owe money for groceries. Find out how much and pay it.”
4. Define the **expected path**: click through the correct screens in Maze’s editor so it can measure direct success.

### 2. Add questions
1. After the mission, add an **Opinion scale** block: “How easy was this task?” (1–5).
2. Add an **Open question** block: “What, if anything, was confusing?”

### 3. Share and collect
1. Click **Start testing** → copy the link.
2. Send it to 10–15 people matching your persona (WhatsApp/LinkedIn).
3. Wait for responses.

### 4. Read the report
1. Look at **direct success** (followed the expected path), **indirect success** (got there another way), **give-ups**, **misclick rate** and **heatmaps** of where people tapped.
2. Combine with your moderated insights: Maze tells you *where* people struggle; moderated sessions tell you *why*.

## Tips

- **Test on phones** whenever possible; desktop clicks differ from thumbs.
- **Pilot once** with a friend to catch broken prototype links.
- **New participants for v2** so they aren’t learning from v1.
- **Don’t rescue** participants too early; note “help needed” honestly.
- **Record the checkpoint** in PM Path: tick “4 of 5 completed without help” only if it’s true.
