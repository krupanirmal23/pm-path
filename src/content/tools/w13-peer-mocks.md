## What it is

A **peer mock** is a practice interview with another aspiring or working PM over a video call (Google Meet is free and familiar). You take turns playing interviewer and candidate, using real interview questions and a shared rubric.

## Why PMs use it

Recording yourself helps, but nothing replaces the pressure and interaction of a live interviewer: follow-up questions, interruptions, hints. Your Week 13 deliverable is 4 live mocks, and your checkpoint is two partners in a row rating you “would advance”. Playing interviewer also teaches you what interviewers notice.

## Account setup (free plan)

1. **Google Meet**: meet.google.com with your Google account. One-to-one calls are free; check current time limits for group calls.
2. **Finding partners:** PM communities (Slack/Discord/WhatsApp groups for aspiring PMs), LinkedIn posts (“Looking for a PM mock interview partner, happy to swap”), college alumni groups, and people you meet during outreach. Some platforms match mock partners; free options exist.

## Walkthrough: run a 60-minute swap

### 1. Before the call
1. Agree on **round types** each person wants to practise (e.g., you: product design; partner: metrics).
2. Each person picks a question for the *other* from PM Path’s Interview Prep (don’t reveal it in advance).
3. Share the rubric (PM Path Mock Interview rubric: structure, user focus, creativity, prioritisation, metrics, communication; 1–4 each; overall verdict: would advance / borderline / no).
4. Create the Meet link; open a shared Google Doc or Excalidraw for whiteboarding.

### 2. Round 1 (25 minutes + 5 feedback)
1. **Interviewer** reads the question and plays the role realistically: answers clarifying questions briefly, gives hints only if the candidate is very stuck, interrupts occasionally (“Let’s skip to metrics”) to test adaptability.
2. **Candidate** answers as in a real interview, sharing the doc/screen for structure.
3. **Interviewer** takes notes: timestamps of strong moments and where they got lost.
4. **Feedback (5 minutes):** interviewer gives scores, verdict, 2 strengths, 2 specific improvements. Candidate listens, asks clarifying questions, doesn’t argue.

### 3. Round 2: swap roles
Repeat with roles reversed.

### 4. After the call
1. Each person logs the mock in their tracker (PM Path: Interview Prep → Mock log) with question, category, scores, verdict and notes.
2. Pick one habit to fix before the next mock.
3. Thank your partner; schedule the next swap (consistency helps both).

## Being a good mock interviewer

- **Stay in character**; don’t coach mid-answer unless agreed.
- **Ask realistic follow-ups:** “Why that segment?”, “What if engineering says it takes 6 months?”, “How would you know it worked?”
- **Give specific feedback:** “You spent 9 minutes clarifying; aim for 3” beats “Be faster.”
- **Be honest with the verdict**; false praise doesn’t help anyone.

## Tips

- **Mix round types** across mocks (design, improvement, estimation, strategy; next week metrics, behavioural and AI PM cases).
- **Practise with working PMs** when you can; they’ll sound like real interviewers.
- **Use video**; it mimics real virtual interviews in India.
- **Track verdicts over time** in PM Path to see progress towards “would advance” consistently.
- **Respect time zones and schedules**; keep sessions to the agreed length.
