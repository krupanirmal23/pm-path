## What it is

**Google Forms** is a free survey tool from Google. You build a form with different question types, share a link, and responses flow into a Google Sheet automatically.

## Why PMs use it

PMs use forms for research screeners, quick surveys, beta sign-ups, feedback after launches, and internal requests. It’s free, familiar to participants and connects straight to Sheets for analysis.

## Account setup (free plan)

1. You need a Google account (Gmail). If you have one, you’re set.
2. Go to forms.google.com and click the **+ Blank form** tile.
3. Everything you need is free: unlimited forms and responses.

## Walkthrough: your P1 screener

Use the participant profile and screener you drafted in the Recruiting lesson.

### 1. Set up the form
1. Click **Untitled form** and rename it: “Quick research: [your topic] (2 minutes)”.
2. In the description, write 2–3 lines: who you are, what you’re learning, that it takes 2 minutes, and that you’ll only use contact details to schedule a call. Example: “I’m learning product management and researching how flatmates handle shared expenses. If you’re a fit, I’ll invite you to a 30-minute call. No selling, ever.”
3. Click the **palette icon** (Customise theme) and pick a simple colour. Keep it plain.

### 2. Add behaviour questions
1. Click **+** (Add question). Change the type with the dropdown on the right.
2. For **ranges**, use **Multiple choice**: “In the last 2 months, how many times did you split an expense with a flatmate?” Options: 0 / 1 / 2–4 / 5+.
3. For **“which of these”** questions, use **Checkboxes**. Always add a **None of these** option.
4. For the **open story question**, use **Paragraph**: “Describe the last time you settled up shared expenses. What happened?”
5. Toggle **Required** on for the key filtering questions.

### 3. Add an exclusion question
1. Add a **Multiple choice**: “Do you work in fintech or at a company that makes expense apps?” Yes / No.
2. Keep it among other questions so it doesn’t stand out.

### 4. Add logistics on a second section
1. Click the **Add section** icon (two stacked rectangles). Title it “Contact (only if you’re open to a call)”.
2. Add: “Open to a 30-minute video call this week?” (Yes/No), “Best times” (Checkboxes: weekday evenings, weekend mornings, etc.), “Name” (Short answer), “Email or WhatsApp number” (Short answer).
3. Optional: use **response validation** (⋮ menu → Response validation) to check that the email field contains an email.

### 5. Settings
1. Click the **Settings** tab. Under **Responses**, leave “Collect email addresses” **off** (you ask for contact details yourself, only from willing people).
2. Under **Presentation**, write a confirmation message: “Thank you! If you’re a fit, I’ll message you within 2 days.”

### 6. Preview and test
1. Click the **eye icon** (Preview). Fill it in yourself. Time it: under 2 minutes?
2. Ask one friend to try it and tell you what they think you’re looking for. If they can guess, make the filtering questions less obvious.

### 7. Share and collect
1. Click **Send** → the **link icon** → tick **Shorten URL** → **Copy**.
2. Paste it into your LinkedIn post and WhatsApp messages.
3. In the **Responses** tab, click **Link to Sheets** to create a response spreadsheet.
4. In the Sheet, add a column **Qualified?** and mark each row Yes/No as responses arrive. Add **Status** (Invited / Scheduled / Done).

## Tips

- **Short is everything.** Every extra question loses respondents.
- **Ask behaviour with ranges**, never “Would you…?”.
- **Use sections** so logistics come last, after people are engaged.
- **Be transparent** about how you’ll use their data, and delete contact details after the project.
- **Re-use the form** for later research by making a copy (⋮ → Make a copy).
