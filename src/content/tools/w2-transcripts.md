## What it is

A **transcript** is a written record of what was said in a conversation. **Google Meet** can show live captions (and, on some accounts, save transcripts). **Otter.ai** is an AI note-taker that records and transcribes meetings, with a free plan that has monthly minute limits.

## Why PMs use it

Transcripts let you listen fully during interviews instead of typing, and give you exact quotes for your report. Every insight in your P1 report needs at least two verbatim quotes, so accurate transcripts make synthesis much faster and more honest.

> [!WARNING]
> **Always ask for consent before recording or transcribing.** Say it at the start, and note the answer. If someone says no, take notes by hand. Never share recordings or transcripts publicly; anonymise quotes (use P1, P2…) in your report.

## Account setup (free plan)

**Option A: Google Meet (simplest)**
1. Use your Google account at meet.google.com.
2. Live captions are free for everyone: click **CC** (Turn on captions) in the bottom bar.
3. Saved transcripts depend on your account type. Many free personal accounts can’t save them, so pair captions with Option B or C.

**Option B: Otter.ai free plan**
1. Go to otter.ai → **Sign up free** with Google.
2. The free plan includes a limited number of transcription minutes per month and a per-conversation length limit. Check the current limits on the pricing page; it’s usually enough for 5–6 short interviews if you keep them to 30 minutes.
3. Install the mobile app if you’ll do any in-person interviews.

**Option C: phone recording + free transcription**
1. Record audio with your phone’s voice recorder (with consent).
2. Upload to a transcription tool, or use Google Docs **Voice typing** (Tools → Voice typing) while playing the audio. It’s slower but free.

## Walkthrough: transcribe a P1 interview

### Before the call
1. Create the Meet link and send it in the calendar invite.
2. In the invite, mention: “With your permission, I’d like to transcribe the call so I can listen properly. I’ll only use anonymous quotes.”
3. If using Otter, connect your Google Calendar in Otter settings so “Otter Assistant” can join the meeting, or plan to start a recording manually from the Otter app.

### At the start of the call
1. Ask: “Is it okay if I record a transcript? It’s only for my notes and I’ll anonymise any quotes.” Wait for a clear yes.
2. Turn on Meet captions (**CC**) and/or start Otter recording.
3. Keep a doc open for **timestamps only**: when something important is said, jot the time and 3 words (“12:40 rounding off ₹600”). Don’t try to type everything.

### Right after the call
1. Write your 5-line summary within 15 minutes: who, key story, top 3 quotes, surprises, follow-ups.
2. Open the transcript. Use your timestamps to jump to key moments.
3. Fix errors in the important quotes. Names, rupee amounts and app names are often mis-transcribed.
4. Copy key lines into a shared “Notes” doc, one line per observation, prefixed with the participant code: `P3: "The cook is cash; groceries are three different apps."`

### Prepare for affinity mapping
1. Aim for 30–60 lines per interview.
2. In Miro, paste the lines into the board; Miro can turn pasted multi-line text into separate sticky notes (see the Miro guide).

## Tips

- **Test the setup** on a call with a friend first.
- **Use headphones** to avoid echo, which ruins transcripts.
- **Ask people to share stories**, not long silences on screen share; transcripts capture words, not clicks. For screen shares, take screenshots.
- **Store transcripts privately** in a Google Drive folder, and delete recordings once the project ends.
- **Never paste identifying details** (names, phone numbers, salaries) into public documents or AI tools.
