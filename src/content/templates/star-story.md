## When to use
To prepare for behavioural questions (“Tell me about a time…”). Build 10 stories and save them in **Interview Prep → Story bank**, tagged by theme (conflict, failure, leadership, data, ambiguity, customer).

## How to fill it
- **Situation (15%):** context in 1–2 sentences.
- **Task (10%):** your specific responsibility.
- **Action (50%):** what *you* did, step by step. Use “I”, not “we”.
- **Result (25%):** outcome with numbers, and what you learned.
- Aim for 2 minutes spoken. One story can answer several questions if you change the emphasis.

# STAR: Changing a launch plan using customer evidence

**Themes:** influence, customer, data

**Situation:** At Palo Alto Networks, a new firewall feature was about to launch with a setup guide that the product team felt was complete.

**Task:** As the Technical Marketing Engineer, I owned the launch demo and customer-facing content, but I had no authority over the product.

**Action:**
1. I ran the setup with 6 field engineers and timed them. Four got stuck at the same policy step, taking 40+ minutes.
2. I grouped their blockers into 3 themes and recorded short clips of where they hesitated.
3. I shared a one-page summary with the PM, suggesting two small changes: a default policy template and a clearer error message.
4. When the PM said the release was locked, I proposed a compromise: ship the template in the guide now and the error message in the next patch.

**Result:** The template went into the launch guide. Setup support tickets for that step were about 20% lower than for the previous feature launch, and the error message shipped a month later.

**Learning:** Bringing evidence and a small, scoped ask works better than arguing for a big change. I now start with “what is the smallest change that solves most of the pain?”
