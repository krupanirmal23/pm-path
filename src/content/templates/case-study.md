## When to use
For each portfolio project (P1–P4). Publish on Notion or a simple site and link it from your resume and LinkedIn.

## How to fill it
1. The **TL;DR** must work alone: problem, what you did, result.
2. Show **decisions and trade-offs**, not just steps. “I chose X over Y because…”
3. Embed artefacts (screens, diagrams, doc links).
4. Use real numbers. If you have none, say what you would measure and why.
5. End with what you learned; it signals growth.

# FlatSplit: helping flatmates settle up without the awkward ask

**TL;DR:** Flatmates settle late because asking for money is awkward. I ran 12 interviews and a 64-person survey, designed a neutral monthly summary with one-tap UPI payment, and tested it with 8 users. Task success for paying went from 4/5 to 3/3 after one iteration.
**Role:** Solo · **Timeline:** 8 weeks · **Tools:** Notion, Figma, Google Forms, Sheets

## The problem
Young professionals sharing flats in Bengaluru track rent, the cook’s salary and groceries over WhatsApp. 9 of 12 people I interviewed settle late or never. “I’d rather lose ₹500 than chase my friend for it.”

## What I did
### 1. Discovery
I expected the problem to be maths. Interviews showed it was social: 7 of 12 avoided asking. That changed the solution from “better tracking” to “a neutral reminder”.
### 2. Decision
I compared three options with RICE: a better logging flow, recurring expenses and a monthly summary. The summary scored highest (6,400) and directly tested the key insight. I chose not to build payment collection to avoid holding money.
### 3. Build / test
Figma prototype tested with 5 people. Three could not tell who had paid. I added “Paid / Not yet” labels and a full-width Pay button; a retest with 3 new people had 100% success.

## Results
- Task success for checking who paid: 2/5 → 3/3.
- Time to pay: 21 s → 11 s.
- Proposed primary metric: % groups fully settled per month (31% baseline from survey).

## What I learned
- Ask about the last time it happened; my first two interviews were too hypothetical.
- Small labels beat icons for status.

## Next steps
- A/B test the summary (plan written, ~1,400 groups per variant).
- Explore AI expense entry (became my P4).
