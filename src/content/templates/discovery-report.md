## When to use
After 5+ interviews and any supporting data, before deciding what to build. This is the P1 Week 2 deliverable.

## How to fill it
1. Lead with the **recommendation** (busy readers stop after the first paragraph).
2. Each insight = pattern + evidence (how many people, a quote) + implication.
3. Include what **surprised** you and what you got wrong; it shows honest research.
4. Finish with what you still don’t know.

# Discovery report: month-end settling for flatmates

**TL;DR:** Flatmates don’t struggle to *track* expenses as much as to *ask for and collect* money. 9 of 12 settle late or never, mostly to avoid awkwardness. We recommend exploring a neutral, app-sent monthly summary with one-tap UPI payment.

## 1. Research goal and method
- Goal: decide whether month-end settling is worth building for.
- Method: 12 interviews (30 min), survey of 64 people via college and office groups, review of 200 Play Store reviews of expense apps.
- Participants: 22–30, sharing flats in Bengaluru, Pune and Hyderabad.

## 2. Key insights
### Insight 1: Awkwardness, not maths, delays settling
- Evidence: 7 of 12 described avoiding asking. “I’d rather lose ₹500 than chase my friend.”
- Implication: a reminder that comes from the app, not a person, could remove social friction.

### Insight 2: Tracking tools die after 1–2 months
- Evidence: 8 of 12 had tried a sheet or app; 6 stopped. Survey: 58% stopped within 2 months.
- Implication: logging must be near-effortless, or value must come even with incomplete data.

### Insight 3: UPI is universal, but amounts are typed manually
- Evidence: all 12 pay via UPI; 10 type the amount by hand and 4 described paying the wrong amount once.
- Implication: pre-filled payment links remove errors and steps.

## 3. Assumptions: confirmed or rejected
| Assumption | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| People don’t know exact amounts | Partly | Organisers know; others don’t |
| Asking is awkward | Confirmed | 7 of 12, strong emotion |
| People churn from tools | Confirmed | 6 of 8 who tried |

## 4. Personas / segments
- **Riya, the organiser:** tracks everything, hates chasing.
- **Arjun, the forgetful payer:** happy to pay, never remembers.

## 5. Opportunity
**How might we** help flatmates settle every month without anyone having to ask?

## 6. Recommendation
Prototype a monthly summary sent by the app with one-tap UPI pay. Test with 5 flats before building logging improvements.

## 7. Open questions
- Will an app reminder feel neutral or naggy? — test with prototype.
- Do bigger flats (4+) behave differently? — 3 more interviews.
